Hello! My name is Anthony Avery Welcher, and I am a Steward at Four Seasons Hotels & Resorts in Westlake Village, CA. Here is a little bit about my background…

Spawned into the world on February 12th 1999, I grew up in the suburban streets of Ventura CA. While I did have fascination in sports such as soccer, baseball and bodyboarding in my youth, my path was forged by a hard-working couple with professions in Oncology—or the study of cancer. 

Being raised in a white-collar household, academics were always the priority. Despite achieving moderate school success in my studies during high school and community college, I was never complacent with taking orders from others. This sentiment was quickly extrapolated through my first official job as a cashier at a local taco shack where the revelation came to me: regardless if a person is making thirty thousand a year, or half a million a year, working a job comes with its caveats; particularly the scheduled sacrifice of one’s most precious asset—time. 

My number one goal in life as a bachelor is to start my own restaurant dedicated to a short, yet hearty, dinner service featured from 5pm-9pm Wednesdays through Sundays. To me, starting a restaurant is the pinnacle of the American Dream. All are qualified to do it, so long as the person is willing to work hard and adapt. Win or lose, being able to leave the world knowing that I actually tried to pursue a life-long dream of mine is a key aspect to my raison d’être.

Starting my own restaurant is special to me because if there is one difference between my parents and me that eclipses all of the other differences, it is that I do not enjoy cooking with recipes. I always enjoyed cooking as a spontaneous and creative outlet, juxtapose to my elders who would follow a NYT cooking recipe to a tee everytime. My belief is that the one life we are given is not meant to be lived by a conventional recipe book, but rather, it is the responsibility of the individual to understand what ingredients they possess in his or her internal grocery basket, and to then craft a unique product using their skills, resources and passions. 

I believe people are like snowflakes, with the origins of each determining how they shape up, and of course with no two being identical. It is this exact philosophy that propels how I work in the kitchen, how I write at the desk, and how I strive to connect with others on a day-to-day basis. Thank you for reading my story!
